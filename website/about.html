01 · MISSION & PRINCIPLES

Our goals

01 / 04

Transforming the grid edge — one building at a time.

As electrical demands surge from data centers and electric mobility, optimizing building energy use is critical for ensuring sustainable smart cities. ThermoVerse optimizes the grid’s most energy-intensive assets to make sustainable urban innovation possible. By redirecting peak HVAC demand, ThermoVerse unlocks the grid capacity necessary to power the next generation of electric mobility and AI-driven infrastructure.

Lowering HVAC Energy Burden Today

Harnessing waste heat for grid-ready building efficiency.

We develop the technologies that harness waste heat to significantly reduce HVAC operational load, ensuring commercial and multifamily buildings are efficient, resilient, and grid-ready for the era of smart cities.

Waste Heat Utilization · Grid-Ready Buildings

A resilient and interoperable energy network.

Our work is vital for addressing rising loads from electric vehicles (EVs) and data centers, ensuring a resilient and interoperable energy network — from the building edge to the grid edge.

From Building Edge to Grid Edge

From ceiling to grid: Smart Integrated Material systems (SIMs).

A patented family of compact, self-contained units that embed sensing, thermal energy storage, and control directly into ceilings and walls. SIMs orchestrate heat flows in real time, minimizing waste, stabilizing indoor environments, and unlocking load flexibility for operators and utilities alike.

02 · COMPANY BACKGROUND

A purpose-driven beginning to alleviate urban grid burden.

Seeing a major opportunity to alleviate grid burden, Shantonio Birch founded ThermoVerse with a bold vision: purpose-driven design that blends efficiency, comfort, and resilience. Rather than passive thermal storage, he set out to create dynamic, intelligently controlled systems that actively dispatch stored thermal energy when and where it’s needed most.

The COVID-19 stay-at-home orders laid bare systemic failings in building performance: poor air quality, widespread heat stress, and wildly variable HVAC efficacy across climate zones. Nearly 1 in 3 U.S. households reported challenges sustaining adequate heating and cooling, and 1 in 10 said they kept their homes at unhealthy or unsafe temperatures, requiring transformative new solutions.
